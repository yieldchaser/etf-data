1 · The thesis

Predator Protocol is built around one idea: institutional conviction is detectable before it becomes consensus.

Smart-Beta ETFs — momentum, quality, value, spinoff — are run by systematic strategies that rebalance on rules. Each one is a different epistemology of "what looks attractive right now." When several of these independent strategies simultaneously accumulate the same name, that convergence is meaningful: it means the name appears in multiple distinct lenses at once.

Predator scrapes 30 such ETFs daily, normalises their holdings, scores each ticker by how broadly & conviction-weightedly it's held, and surfaces the names where conviction is rising fastest.

What this is NOT: a factor-decomposition engine. There is no regression against macro/style factors, no factor-of-the-day, no purified residuals. Predator measures institutional accumulation breadth and conviction — that is the entire product.

3 · The Conviction Score

For every (ETF, ticker) pair, we compute a single-holding score; the leaderboard's Final Alpha Score is the sum of those across all ETFs holding the ticker. The current formula is the conviction-weighted mode (the legacy weight×rank model is preserved for backward compat).

3.1 — The two ingredients

Each holding contributes proportionally to two factors:

  • Overweight (Wovr) — how much more weight this ETF assigns the name vs equal-weight. OW = weight × n_holdings; OW = 1 means equal-weight, OW = 5 means 5× equal-weight. We compress with ln(1 + OW) / ln(1 + OW★) so that OW★ = 4 anchors Wovr ≈ 1.0, then clamp to [0, 1.5].
  • Rank percentile (Grank) — where the ticker ranks within the ETF. Top of book = 1.0, bottom = 0.0. We add a 0.35 floor so a position never fully zeroes out: Grank = 0.35 + 0.65 × RP.
Single Score  = TierPoints × Wovr(OW) × Grank(RP)
Final Score   = Σ (Single Score) across all ETFs holding this ticker

3.2 — Why this shape

The shape is deliberately additive across ETFs (so breadth matters) but multiplicative within each ETF (so an oversized #2 weight in a small fund counts more than a tiny #50 weight in a large fund). The OW★ anchor and 1.5 clamp keep extreme outliers from dominating; the rank floor keeps a #100 holding from becoming worthless when it might still be a real signal.

4 · Motion signals

Score levels measure where conviction is. Motion signals measure where conviction is going. Three are surfaced on the leaderboard:

4.1 — Velocity score

velocity = 0.5 × global_rank_Δ_30d
         + 0.25 × peak_improvement_30d
         + 1.0  × avg_per_etf_rank_Δ_7d
         + 20.0 × avg_weight_flow_7d
         + 5.0  × etfs_added_30d
         + 1.0  × score_streak

A composite picking up both steady accumulation (rising rank, growing weight, more ETFs picking it up) and burst moves (peak rank improvement). The coefficients are tuning, not theory; the goal is a sortable scalar that ranks "moving up fast" reliably.

4.2 — BURST flag

A name is flagged BURST when all three of these hold for the trailing 30 days:

  • Peak global-rank improvement ≥ 40 positions
  • Continuous presence in ≥ 80% of the daily snapshots in the window (so we're not flagging a one-day blip)
  • Rank better than the within-window median for ≥ 8 of the last 10 snapshots (so the move is sustained, not a fluke)

4.3 — Conviction divergence

Surfaces names where score is rising but rank is falling (being crowded out by faster movers — a "leaking" position) or the inverse (rank rising while score falls — relative-strength-on-cheap).

4.4 — Extreme conviction moves (homepage ribbon)

On the leaderboard, the top strip surfaces names whose motion metrics are statistically unusual (|z| ≥ 2) vs the leaderboard's own distribution today. Pure presentation — every value is already in the leaderboard JSON. Quality gate: requires ≥ 3 ETFs holding (so a single-ETF data hiccup can't surface a fake outlier).

6 · Sanitizer

Before scoring runs, holdings rows are filtered & normalised:

  • Currency placeholders blocked — $USD, $BRL, $JPY, etc.
  • Money-market funds blocked by exact name match (Short-Term Investment Trust …) and substring (Money Market, Securities Lending).
  • Ticker punctuation standardised — BRK-B → BRK.B, BF/B → BF.B, whitespace collapsed.
  • Dual-class consolidation — GOOG → GOOGL (otherwise dual-class shares fragment the leaderboard).
  • Cross-listing collapse — Korean A-prefix tickers (A005930 → 005930) merged so Samsung Electronics doesn't appear twice with split conviction.

7 · Maturity gate (§28)

An ETF that has been tracked for fewer than 30 days is "immature." Immature ETFs contribute to scoring but don't trigger NEW-entrant flags. Without this, a freshly-onboarded ETF would flag every position it holds as NEW — drowning the changelog with noise from a data-engineering event rather than market action.

8 · Self-living markets data

The Markets page (long-horizon return matrix, periodic table, drawdowns, etc.) is built from a layered data source:

  1. Excel deep-history baseline — Mega_Markets_Historical.xlsx seeds the JSON with multi-century data (gold from 1833, S&P 500 from 1871, Dow Jones from 1885, etc.). It runs once per build as a backfill seed.
  2. Live FRED + yfinance merge — predator.markets_history extends every series with fresh monthly closes and populates fx (USD/INR via DEXINUS, etc.), CPI (CPIAUCSL), and rates (10Y, 2Y, Fed Funds…). New months overwrite the Excel baseline; live source labels are preserved.

The build emits metadata.json with a markets_data_freshness block that an outside reader can verify directly:

"markets_data_freshness": {
  "asof":          "2026-05",
  "by_source":     {"yfinance": 18, "fred": 9, "excel": 5},
  "self_living_check": {
    "live_source_count": 27,
    "excel_only_count":  5,
    "verdict": "LIVE_MERGE_HEALTHY"
  }
}

If the verdict is LIVE_MERGE_HEALTHY, the live merge is doing its job: most assets carry a live FRED / yfinance source label, fx / cpi / rates are populated, and the Markets page's currency lens & real-returns toggles work. If it ever reads LIVE_MERGE_FAILED, the FRED step is failing in CI and the live merge needs attention. Per-step continue-on-error guarantees one upstream hiccup never collapses the whole build.

9 · The permanent history archive

Holdings history is partitioned by year at data/history_parquet/year=YYYY/holdings.parquet. The companion file CHECKSUMS.json records a SHA-256 of each partition.

  • Past-year partitions are immutable. The migrate script refuses to rewrite them; a CI test recomputes the SHA-256s on every build and fails if any prior-year partition has drifted.
  • Current-year partition is mutable (it grows daily as new scrapes land).
  • To intentionally fix a historical mistake, the operator must run with --allow-historical-rewrite; that flag logs a loud warning and updates the manifest.

10 · Honest limitations

  • Predator measures detection breadth, not future returns. A high score means many independent strategies converge on a name; it does not mean the name will outperform. Backtests of HC-entry, BURST-trigger, and Top-N-Velocity strategies are on the Backtest page — judge for yourself.
  • The system is one source. No earnings, no fundamentals, no macro overlay. Use it alongside other tools, not instead of them.
  • Daily granularity, not intraday. Scrapes run 14:00 + 22:00 UTC weekdays. Same-day moves are not captured.
  • Some positions are hidden. Equity-only filtering removes cash, derivatives, money-market, and currency placeholders. You see the equity book, not the full ETF.
  • Tier weights are editorial. The 40 / 60 / 30 / 10 / 2 numbers are calibrated by hand. The current values are pinned by regression tests (Samsung > Itaú in conviction mode); changes require a measured before/after.
  • Cold-start gaps. Each ETF has a 30-day maturity ramp during which NEW flags are suppressed. The system started in February 2026; the leaderboard reaches its full signal density once every ETF is past the maturity gate.
