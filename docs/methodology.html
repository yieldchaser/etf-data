1 · The thesis

Conviction Labs is built around one idea: institutional conviction is detectable before it becomes consensus.

Smart-Beta ETFs — momentum, quality, value, spinoff — are run by systematic strategies that rebalance on rules. Each one is a different epistemology of "what looks attractive right now." When several of these independent strategies simultaneously accumulate the same name, that convergence is meaningful: it means the name appears in multiple distinct lenses at once.

Conviction Labs scrapes the configured ETF universe daily — see the live count in the dashboard header — normalises their holdings, scores each ticker by how broadly & conviction-weightedly it's held, and surfaces the names where conviction is rising fastest.

What this is NOT: a factor-decomposition engine. There is no regression against macro/style factors, no factor-of-the-day, no purified residuals. Conviction Labs measures institutional accumulation breadth and conviction — that is the entire product.

3 · The Conviction Score (v4)

For every (ETF, ticker) pair we compute a per-holding conviction, square it, weight it by the fund's tier points, and sum across funds. Three structural multipliers then shape that mass into the Conviction Score — the one canonical score and rank on the leaderboard. v4 (2026-10) is the result of a walk-forward study on the real store; the evidence for every change is in SCORING_V4_STUDY.md and §3.5 below.

3.1 — Per-holding conviction

Each holding's conviction Ci is the product of two factors:

  • Overweight (Wovr) — how much more weight this ETF assigns the name vs equal-weight. OW = weight × n_holdings; OW = 1 means equal-weight, OW = 5 means 5× equal-weight. We compress with ln(1 + OW) / ln(1 + OW★) so that OW★ = 4 anchors Wovr ≈ 1.0, then clamp to [0, 1.5].
  • Rank percentile (Grank) — where the ticker ranks within the ETF. Top of book = 1.0, bottom = 0.0. A 0.35 floor keeps a position from fully zeroing out: Grank = 0.35 + 0.65 × RP.
Ci          = Wovr(OWi) × Grank(RPi)
Singlei     = TierPointsi × Ciκ          (κ = 2)
B             = Σ Singlei across holding ETFs  (overlap-discounted, §3.4)
Score         = B × Mconc × Mbreadth × Muniverse

3.2 — Why this shape

Additive across ETFs (so breadth matters), multiplicative within each ETF (so an oversized #2 weight in a small fund counts more than a tiny #50 weight in a large fund). The convexity κ = 2 is what makes it breadth of conviction rather than breadth of filler: a fund carrying a name at C = 0.3 (below equal weight, low in the book) contributes 0.09 of a full position, while a genuine overweight at C = 1.2 contributes 1.44. Six funds' worth of filler no longer outscores two funds that put the name at the top of their books.

Because the discount is applied per holding, the score is monotone in breadth: a new holder can only add to a name's score. The averaging-based corrections it replaces (§3.5) did not have that property — under them, a strong single-fund name that picked up seven filler holders could lose score (57.6 → 45 in a worked example).

3.3 — Structural multipliers

  • Mconc (0.60–1.00) — concentration guard. Effective fund count Neff = 1/HHI of per-fund score shares; the penalty fully releases at 3 effective funds. The floor is 0.60 — a single fund's massive bet is dampened, never hidden.
  • Mbreadth (1.00–1.35) — strategy dispersal. Shannon entropy of the score across the 5 tiers — a name confirmed by Quality AND Scout simultaneously outranks the same mass coming from one style. This is the only breadth reward in the score.
  • Muniverse (0.80–1.25) — opportunity normalization. Score per unit of demonstrated opportunity (Σ points of every ETF that has ever held the name, plus shrinkage), clipped so it can level the field for international names without fabricating signal. This is the only regional levelling in the score.

Scores are reported to one decimal (they were truncated to integers before v4; under κ = 2 a held filler is worth fractions of a point, and truncation collapsed almost half the board to 0). Legacy and conviction modes remain available via config for backward comparison; κ applies in apex mode only.

3.4 — ETF overlap discount

When several ETFs belong to the same strategy cluster (e.g. JHEM, MFEM and EEMO, sharing up to 35% of holdings), additive scoring can double-count duplicate allocator conviction. The engine computes pairwise Jaccard similarity of fund holdings; for a ticker held by several funds, each subsequent (lower-scoring) fund's contribution is scaled by max(0.20, 1.0 − 1.5 × max_similarity) against the funds already counted.

3.5 — What changed in v4 (2026-10) and why

A walk-forward study replayed the real engine on every snapshot date (Feb–Oct 2026) and scored each candidate change on forward returns (T+2 entry, 5–40 trading days, currency-demeaned) and on internal outcomes: the share of breadth-filler names in the top 50, whether the score predicts future fund accumulation, how often top-50 names lose a holder within 30 days, and rank stability. Results, 30-ETF universe:

pre-v4v4
Top-50 breadth-filler share (avg conviction < 0.5)17.6%4.8%
Forward fund-accumulation IC (score vs 30d change in holders)−0.009+0.018
Top-50 names losing a holder within 30d18.8%14.4%
Top-50 still top-100 after ~28d92.1%93.7%
20-day forward-return IC (unchanged — see §11)−0.031−0.031

The 16-ETF universe (Feb–May, out-of-sample) confirms the direction: filler share 53% → 27%, accumulation IC +0.002 → +0.046, exit rate 15.9% → 11.2%.

  • Added — κ = 2 per-holding convexity (§3.2).
  • Removed — tier-synergy bonus (1 + 0.15 × (tiers − 1)). It rewarded tier breadth on top of Mbreadth, so a 5-tier name earned up to 1.60 × 1.35 = 2.16× from breadth alone — the main source of the "point moat" that kept large multi-fund names pinned at the top.
  • Removed — 60-point international exception. A second regional-levelling mechanism stacked on Muniverse; combined with κ = 2 it would have pushed the top 50 to 76% non-US names (vs 56% before v4 and 41.5% of holding-points). With uniform tier points the top 50 sits at 60%.
  • Removed — APEX kicker, m_conv and the conviction gate (formerly §3.3–3.4): see §5.1. apex_score / apex_rank remain in the data as aliases of the Conviction Score so downstream consumers don't break.
  • Added — security-identity resolution and a staleness guard (§6).

4 · Motion signals

Score levels measure where conviction is. Motion signals measure where conviction is going. The hand-tuned velocity composite was retired in the 2026-08 signal study (see §5.1 Retired signals); what ships today is the raw rank-trajectory layer plus the empirically evaluated flags below.

4.1 — Rank trajectory metrics

Per ticker, computed over trailing windows and consumed directly by the UI and by the Pre-HC / Stealth-Buy filters:

  • global_rank_delta_30d / global_rank_peak_30d — net and best rank improvement across the 30-day window (positive = improved), with a ≥ 80%-of-window coverage check so one-day blips don't count.
  • avg_rank_delta_7d / avg_rank_delta_30d — mean per-ETF rank change across the funds currently holding the name.
  • avg_weight_flow_7d — mean per-ETF weight change over the trailing 7 days.
  • etf_count_delta_30d — net ETF positions added or removed over 30 days. Sector/country flow aggregations now measure the same thing: the flow.json keys are net_funds_delta (sum) and avg_funds_delta (mean) of per-name ETF-count change.

4.2 — BURST — a fund-confirmed rank surge (redesigned 2026-10)

On the leaderboard rank smoothed by a 3-snapshot rolling median (so a one-day spike cannot move it), over the last 10 snapshots (~2 weeks), a name is flagged BURST when all of these hold:

  • its rank improved by at least ×1.65 (log 0.5; e.g. #250 → #150) — a rank-relative move, so it means the same at #50 as at #250;
  • it is now inside the top 300 (moves deep in the board are churn, not signal);
  • it is still within 15% of its best rank in the window (the move is in force, not already given back);
  • it gained at least one holding fund over the same window.
Why it was rebuilt (2026-10 signal study): the old rule measured the range of the raw rank over 30 days (worst − best ≥ 40) with no direction, so a one-day glitch from a partial ETF scrape (#100 → #140 → #100) qualified, and a name that only fell showed a large "peak improvement". It flagged ~870 names a day, median rank ~1,700, with no forward edge (−0.5% vs peers). Fund-confirmed BURST flags ~6–8 names a day; onsets beat currency peers by +5.2% over 20 days in the momentum-friendly Feb–May regime and were flat (−0.9%, not significant) in the May–Oct momentum unwind, where an unconfirmed rank surge lost 2.6%. Details: SIGNALS_STUDY.md.

4.3 — CRATER — the get-out signal (redesigned 2026-10)

The mirror of BURST, measured over ~4 weeks (20 snapshots) rather than BURST's ~2: the smoothed rank worsened by at least ×1.35, starting from inside the top 300, is still near its worst, and the name lost at least one holding fund over the same 20 snapshots. The longer window exists because a fund's exit often comes before the slide. PIE sold Yageo (2327) at #6 at the start of July; its rank fell ×1.65 only about 13 snapshots later, as the price fell 55% from its June peak, and a 10-snapshot CRATER never fired. CRATER onsets lagged currency peers by −2.9% (May–Oct, t −2.8) and −1.0% (Feb–May) over 20 days. It is one of only two motion rules negative in both regimes, the other being the stricter ×1.65 version (−2.4% / −1.3%), which fired about 20% less. The same widening hurt BURST, which keeps its 10-snapshot window. The old CRATER flagged ~590 names a day and its names went on to outperform.

4.3b — Price-driven grade of BURST / CRATER (SURGE / SLIDE in the data, 2026-10)

The site shows BURST and CRATER in two grades. Fund-backed (solid badge, ✓ in the column) is the rule above. Price-driven (dashed badge, dim number) is a smoothed-rank move of at least ×1.4 over ~2 weeks inside the top 300, up or down, with no fund joining or leaving (momentum_min_log_rank_move = 0.336; stored as surge_10d / slide_10d). A fund's weight in a name drifts with its price, so this is the price move showing through the funds' books. It is the confirming, in-sync-with-the-chart feel of the old BURST / CRATER, restricted to the top 300 and smoothed so a one-day scrape glitch can't trigger it. ×1.4 was chosen so that on a typical day of the 30-fund history ~25 names are bursting and ~40 cratering (at ×1.65 it was ~11 / ~27, which read as "broken").

It confirms a move; it does not predict one. Like every rule built from price, rank or fund re-weighting alone (20 were tested), its next-20-day result flipped sign between regimes: at ×1.65, SURGE names beat peers by +2.9% in Feb–May and lagged by −2.6% in Jun–Oct; SLIDE was −1.7% in Jun–Oct and +0.9% in Feb–May. Timing the regime from the rules' own recent record failed. A price-driven Crater turns fund-backed if a fund exits; that is the step that matters. A name that is fund-backed is not also shown as price-driven.

Why the old flags felt right and weren't: the old BURST / CRATER flagged ~580 names a day, 88–93% of them below #300. Flagged names had moved +4.0% / −3.5% vs peers over the previous 10 days and +0.6% / +0.1% over the next 20. They described moves rather than anticipating them. Full tables: docs/SIGNALS_STUDY.md §3, follow-up 2.

4.4 — NEW

First appearance in an ETF within the 14-day lookback (new_lookback_days), suppressed for ETFs younger than the 30-day maturity baseline (§7). A factual event rather than a call: new entrants to momentum-run funds are momentum names, so NEW members beat currency peers by +6.2% over 20 days in the momentum-friendly Feb–May regime and lagged by 2.3% in the May–Oct unwind.

4.5 — Screening chips (2026-10 audit)

Every chip is computed in the build (history.screening_signals), so each has one tested definition shared by every page. Each was replayed on every snapshot and kept only when its 20-day return vs currency peers had the same sign in both regimes (Feb–May / May–Oct):

ChipRule20d vs peers
Funds ↓A top-200 name lost a holding fund in the last 14 days−2.7% / −7.0% — the steadiest warning in the data
Funds ↑A top-200 name gained a holding fund in the last 30 days+6.2% / +1.5%
Pre-HCOne step from HIGH_CONVICTION and improving: 3+ holders, 1+ conviction holder, 2+ strategy tiers, per-fund ranks and score rising+7.4% / +0.1%
StealthA second independent fund joined the name in the last 14 days (top 500): consensus starting to form+4.9% / +1.7%
Quality+A Quality-tier fund added the name in the last 30 days+0.4% / +0.8%

Selling shows up within two weeks; buying accumulates over a month — hence the different windows. The relevance floor (top 200 / 500) carries most of the gain: deep-board names churn without consequence. Holder-count changes count a name that was not on the board as having had zero holders.

4.6 — CONCENTRATED — a risk descriptor

One fund supplies ≥ 85% of the name's linear conviction mass Σ points × C (or a lone fund holds it at ≥ 4× equal weight); read on the linear mass, not the κ-squared score, so the calibrated threshold keeps its meaning. It tells you the score rests on one fund. It is not a buy signal: CONCENTRATED members showed no edge in the May–Oct regime and lagged peers by 2.0% in Feb–May, so the 2026-08 "best risk-adjusted flag" description was withdrawn.

4.7 — HIGH_CONVICTION — broad, convinced and independent (redesigned 2026-10)

A name is HIGH_CONVICTION when it is held by ≥ 4 ETFs, of which ≥ 2 non-benchmark funds hold it at conviction ≥ 0.6 (≈ 2× equal weight near the top of the book), spanning ≥ 2 non-benchmark strategy tiers. The Core tier (XLG, QQQM) is a cap-weighted benchmark that "overweights" the largest companies by construction, so it counts neither as conviction nor as an independent lens.

Why: "≥ 4 holders" flagged ~38 names in the 16-fund universe but ~200 in the 30-fund one, because several funds hold 500–1,300 names each. The new rule flags ~20–25 (e.g. SNDK, GEV, MU, AAPL), and its names lose a holder within 30 days 15% of the time against 27% under the raw count — the consensus holds. HC names carry more momentum exposure, so their returns follow the momentum regime: +7.3% vs peers in Feb–May, −2.3% in the May–Oct unwind. Each leaderboard row carries conviction_etf_count and strategy_tier_count. Legacy scoring mode keeps the raw-count rule; the 2026-08 gate removed then (θ = 0.8 with ≥ 4 conviction holders) was far stricter and almost never fired.

4.8 — Momentum regime

momentum_regime keeps its five labels (accelerating / rising / stable / weakening / declining), built from the score_streak direction combined with the mean 7-day per-ETF rank delta (avg_rank_delta_7d). It is a display label: in the 2026-10 audit only "declining" leaned negative in both regimes, and weakly. The score-velocity diagnostics (velocity_z / ignition_z) were removed from the payload with the APEX kicker they fed (§5.1).

5.1 — Retired signals (transparency)

Removed from the engine, payloads, and UI in 2026-08, after a 156-snapshot walk-forward study on the real store. We retire signals that don't earn their column:

  • velocity_score — its components cancelled each other: top-quintile forward lift ≤ 1.0 at the 7/14/30-day horizons. The underlying motion columns stay (§4.1); Pre-HC / Stealth-Buy use them directly.
  • stealth_accumulation — only 126 events in 6 months; too rare to act on. Succeeded by the Stealth-Buy chip (§4.6).
  • conviction_divergence — exit-lift pointed the wrong direction (0.56×).
  • quality_defected_30d — lift flipped sign between evaluation grids; unstable. Quality+ adoption remains.

Retired or redefined in the 2026-10 signal audit (SIGNALS_STUDY.md §4) — each replayed on every snapshot and scored on its 20-day return vs currency peers in both regimes:

  • SPECULATIVE_BETA — no edge in either regime (+0.4% / −0.3%); its premise (momentum without a fundamentals lens is speculative) was not borne out.
  • Q+Q chip (Quant + Quality + recent pickup) — sign flipped between regimes (−2.3% / +0.9%).
  • EARLY / CROWDED badges — EARLY flagged ~820 names a day (half the board); CROWDED never fired.
  • ACCUM badge — pure momentum (+3.5% / −1.6%); Funds ↑ covers the idea with an edge in both regimes.
  • "Extreme conviction moves" ribbon — driven by deep-board rank churn (e.g. "+1,902 ranks"); no edge. Replaced by a strip of today's validated signals.
  • ≤ 80% concentration filter — a convenience filter, not a signal; the CONC chip covers the same cut.
  • Old Stealth-Buy (≤ 2 funds, +10% weight in 7 days) — ~250 names a day, mostly price drift in small positions; redefined as a second fund joining (§4.5).
  • Old Pre-HC (exactly 3 funds, improving) and old Funds ↑/↓ (any name, 30 days) — no consistent edge; redefined (§4.5). The old Funds ↓ pointed the wrong way (+2.0% in May–Oct).
  • Old BURST / CRATER (range ≥ 40 ranks) and the raw ≥ 4-holder HIGH_CONVICTION — redesigned (§4.2, §4.3, §4.7).

Retired in 2026-10 (v4 scoring study, §3.5), alongside proposals that were tested and rejected:

  • APEX momentum kicker (× (1 + 0.25·tanh(v_z/2) + 0.10·tanh(i_z/2))) — changed forward-return IC by < 0.001 at every horizon, i.e. noise. The velocity diagnostics stay.
  • m_conv and the conviction gate — two separate clip(avg_conviction, …) factors applied on top of each other (≈ conviction² for fillers), on an unweighted mean in which a 2-point Core fund counted as much as a 40-point Scout fund, and non-monotone in breadth. Replaced by the per-holding convexity κ.
  • Tested, not adopted — 30-day drawdown dampener (strip Mbreadth when a name falls > 20% in 21 trading days). No measurable change in forward-return IC; top-100 names that had fallen > 20% lost a holder less often than other top-100 names (5.9% vs 19.5%), so the "falling knife" premise didn't hold.
  • Tested, not adopted — multi-fund outflow decay. Once a holding's weight change is netted against its own price drift (passive drift isn't selling), the trigger almost never fires; zero measurable effect.
  • Tested, not adopted — diminishing returns on breadth (k-th largest contribution × dk−1). Similar internal gains to κ = 2 with worse rank stability; κ was simpler and better.

6 · Sanitizer

Holdings rows are filtered & normalised at two layers — first at scrape time, then before scoring:

  • Scrape-time exclusion (2026-08) — rows with non-positive weights, $-prefixed currency lines, and Cash&Other aggregates never enter the store; they are dropped the moment a holdings table is parsed.
  • Currency placeholders blocked at scoring — $USD, $BRL, $JPY, etc. (belt-and-braces behind the scrape-time filter).
  • Money-market funds blocked by exact name match (Short-Term Investment Trust …) and substring (Money Market, Securities Lending).
  • Ticker punctuation standardised — BRK-B → BRK.B, BF/B → BF.B, whitespace collapsed.
  • Dual-class consolidation — GOOG → GOOGL (otherwise dual-class shares fragment the leaderboard).
  • Cross-listing collapse — Korean A-prefix tickers (A005930 → 005930) merged so Samsung Electronics doesn't appear twice with split conviction.
  • Share-class consolidation (v4) — voting / non-voting / preferred lines of one issuer pool into the most widely held line: Samsung pref 005935 → 005930, Petrobras PETR3 → PETR4, Itaú ITUB3 → ITUB4, Hyundai prefs → 005380, FOX → FOXA, NWS → NWSA and others (curated list in config.yaml).
  • Security-identity resolution (v4) — international funds report bare local-exchange codes that collide with unrelated US tickers. Before v4, MTN was Vail Resorts (AVSC, JHSC) and MTN Group (JHEM, MFEM) on one row — a fabricated 4-fund HIGH_CONVICTION name in the top 20 — and about 50 other tickers had the same problem (KTB, A, TEL, G, KEY, PEP, BA…). The engine now clusters each ticker's holders by the company names they report; when a US cluster and an international cluster disagree, the international one becomes <ticker>.<TAG> (MTN Group → MTN.MTN). In the other direction, exchange-suffix dialects of one security (6857 vs 6857.JP for Advantest, Thai ADVANC / ADVANC.F / ADVANC.R) merge into one row. Every merge and split must agree with the reported names, so e.g. ENR.GR (Siemens Energy) is never folded into ENR (Energizer). A short curated list handles collisions between two international exchanges (e.g. 2313 = Compeq in Taiwan, Shenzhou in Hong Kong). Each build logs every split, merge and override.
  • Partial-snapshot guard (2026-10) — a fund snapshot whose weight sum (after cash rows are removed) is below 90% of that fund's median over its previous 10 snapshots was scraped incompletely, and is skipped so the fund's last complete book stays in force. Without it, EEMO's 2026-08-29 partial scrape (72% of weight, SK hynix missing) dropped SK hynix from #3 to #34 for two days and fired fake BURST/CRATER moves. 15 such snapshots exist in the store; genuine reconstitutions (fewer names, weights ≈ 100%) pass untouched.
  • Staleness guard (v4) — an ETF whose newest snapshot is more than 30 days older than the freshest snapshot stops scoring, instead of contributing a frozen book forever. The 30-day window is well above the longest observed scrape gap (21 days), so outages never fake an exit.

7 · Maturity gate (§28)

An ETF that has been tracked for fewer than 30 days is "immature." Immature ETFs contribute to scoring but don't trigger NEW-entrant flags. Without this, a freshly-onboarded ETF would flag every position it holds as NEW — drowning the changelog with noise from a data-engineering event rather than market action.

8 · Self-living markets data

The Markets page (long-horizon return matrix, periodic table, drawdowns, etc.) is built from a layered data source:

  1. Excel deep-history baseline — Mega_Markets_Historical.xlsx seeds the JSON with multi-century data (gold from 1833, S&P 500 from 1871, Dow Jones from 1885, etc.). It runs once per build as a backfill seed.
  2. Live FRED + yfinance merge — conviction.markets_history extends every series with fresh monthly closes and populates fx (USD/INR via DEXINUS, etc.), CPI (CPIAUCSL), and rates (10Y, 2Y, Fed Funds…). New months overwrite the Excel baseline; live source labels are preserved.

The build emits metadata.json with a markets_data_freshness block that an outside reader can verify directly:

"markets_data_freshness": {
  "asof":          "2026-05",
  "by_source":     {"yfinance": 18, "fred": 9, "excel": 5},
  "self_living_check": {
    "live_source_count": 27,
    "excel_only_count":  5,
    "verdict": "LIVE_MERGE_HEALTHY"
  }
}

If the verdict is LIVE_MERGE_HEALTHY, the live merge is doing its job: most assets carry a live FRED / yfinance source label, fx / cpi / rates are populated, and the Markets page's currency lens & real-returns toggles work. If it ever reads LIVE_MERGE_FAILED, the FRED step is failing in CI and the live merge needs attention. Per-step continue-on-error guarantees one upstream hiccup never collapses the whole build.

9 · The permanent history archive

Holdings history is partitioned by year at data/history_parquet/year=YYYY/holdings.parquet. The companion file CHECKSUMS.json records a SHA-256 of each partition.

  • Past-year partitions are immutable. The migrate script refuses to rewrite them; a CI test recomputes the SHA-256s on every build and fails if any prior-year partition has drifted.
  • Current-year partition is mutable (it grows daily as new scrapes land).
  • To intentionally fix a historical mistake, the operator must run with --allow-historical-rewrite; that flag logs a loud warning and updates the manifest.

10.1 — Universe expansion (May 2026)

The tracked universe grew in stages. Absolute ranks are universe-relative, so numbers from before and after a jump are not directly comparable:

  • Rank dilution. Doubling the tracked universe mechanically depresses rank numbers after each jump — a top-10 name in a 16-ETF world can sit at #25 in a 30-ETF world without anything "happening" to it. Rank deltas that span late May therefore mix two universes; treat them as qualitative only.
  • Scores are less affected. They're additive per holding, so they survive universe growth better — but breadth-dependent terms (tier breadth, HC count, entropy multipliers) still shift at each boundary.
  • The maturity gate (§7) suppresses NEW-flag floods from freshly added ETFs — onboarding a fund doesn't flag every position it holds as NEW.
  • Charts annotate the break. Every build emits metadata.universe_expansion (the largest single jump plus per-date fund counts), which the dashboards use to mark the boundary.
  • The history window starts at the 30-fund universe (2026-05-22). Every per-stock history the site shows — rank and score charts, the signal timeline, percentile of own history, HC streak, best rank — and every lookback in the Changes tab begins on or after history.universe_start, so nothing compares a 16-fund board with a 30-fund one. The window then grows with each build up to leaderboard_lookback_days (400) and rolls from there. Signals need far less history (BURST 10 snapshots, CRATER 20), so they are unaffected. The 16-fund era stays in the archive and in the research studies, which analyse it as its own regime.
  • YTD in 2026 means "since 2026-05-22". The archive starts on 2026-02-12, so a 1 January baseline never existed, and the YTD score change was blank all year. It is now measured from the universe start, and becomes true year-to-date from January 2027. The YTD buttons show the baseline date on hover.

10.2 — Scraper fail-loud guarantees

The scraper exits 1 on a missing or corrupt config and when zero enabled primary ETFs scrape successfully (a systemic outage must never produce a green run). Partial failures stay warnings — fault isolation across funds is by design, and cron redundancy covers transient blips. Consequence: a green build means data actually flowed; silence is never success.

10b · Fund Flows tab (leveraged ETFs)

Daily creations and redemptions for 160 leveraged, inverse and benchmark ETFs since 2016. The pipeline is automatic. On weekdays, update_daily_flows.py fetches new rows and computes z-scores, 5- and 20-day sums, regime and pressure. Every site build then runs build_local_flow_artifacts.py, which recomputes each fund's record and the pooled evidence.

  • Setups. The scanner tracks four setups on long funds only:
    • inflows after a drop;
    • outflows after a drop;
    • inflows after a rally;
    • outflows after a rally.
    A fund is in a setup when its 20-day price z (|z| ≥ 1, the 20-day return over 60-day volatility) and its 5-day flow z (|z| ≥ 0.8) both stand out against the fund's own history.
  • Evidence. Each setup is shown with its pooled next-20-day result, compared with funds that had the same price move on the same date, for each era (2016–20, 2021–23, 2024–now). The figures are recomputed on every build (catalog.flow_evidence), and the verdict text is generated from them. So far only inflows after a drop has a consistent edge, and it is a small one. No flow setup reliably marks a top.
  • Per-fund record. A fund's own record compares the 20 days after each past setup with that fund's typical 20 days. Episodes must be at least 20 days apart, and the record is shown only once there are 8 or more. The Studio's flow-spike card works the same way over 5 days.
  • Data hygiene.
    • Sometimes a large creation is followed the next day by an almost equal redemption. Each day must be at least 5× typical and the two must cancel to within 20%. This is a source reporting error. It is netted out of the derived metrics and marked ⚠ in the 1-day columns, which rank it as zero.
    • A 1-day flow of 50% or more of AUM is also marked ⚠.
    • Bull vs bear positioning is bull-fund flow minus bear-fund flow.

Full study: scripts/flow_study.py and docs/SIGNALS_STUDY.md §5. Flows are context for choosing among dips, not a standalone trade signal.

11 · Honest limitations

  • Conviction Labs measures detection breadth, not future returns. A high score means many independent strategies converge on a name; it does not mean the name will outperform. Backtests of HC-entry, BURST-trigger, and CRATER strategies are on the Backtest page — judge for yourself.
  • For returns, the score behaves like a momentum exposure. Most of the tracked funds select on momentum, so the score's forward-return information comes and goes with the momentum regime: 20-day IC ≈ +0.05 in Feb–May 2026, ≈ −0.03 in May–Oct 2026, with plain 12-1 price momentum moving the same way. After controlling for 12-1 momentum the residual IC is ≈ ±0.015 and flips sign between periods. No formula change tested in the v4 study moved forward-return IC beyond noise; v4's gains are in what the board is (conviction rather than filler, real identities) and in what it predicts about future fund behaviour.
  • "Overweight" is measured against equal weight, not a benchmark. In a cap-weighted fund the largest companies naturally carry large OW, so part of Ci is company size rather than active conviction — which is why EM mega-caps such as TSMC and Samsung sit near the top. A size-neutral measure needs market caps for the whole universe; the current metadata covers ~1,200 of ~3,900 names.
  • The system is one source. No earnings, no fundamentals, no macro overlay. Use it alongside other tools, not instead of them.
  • Daily granularity, not intraday. Scrapes run 02:00 / 14:00 / 22:00 UTC weekdays plus a weekend catch-up. Same-day moves are not captured.
  • Some positions are hidden. Equity-only filtering removes cash, derivatives, money-market, and currency placeholders. You see the equity book, not the full ETF.
  • Tier weights are editorial. The 40 / 30 / 10 / 2 numbers are calibrated by hand and pinned by regression tests (Samsung > Itaú, every fund earns its tier's points); changes require a measured before/after — the v4 removal of the 60-point exception is documented in §3.5.
  • Multipliers are calibrated to this configured ETF universe. For example, the 0.85 concentration-dominance threshold for CONCENTRATED is read on linear conviction mass and keeps the pre-v4 firing rate (~200 names). A different universe needs recalibration.
  • The forward-return evidence is short. Five months of the 30-ETF universe are roughly four to five independent 20-day windows; v4 was chosen on structural grounds plus internal outcomes that agree in both universes, not on a return backtest.
  • History spans two universes. The May 2026 expansion (see §10 Data notes) means rank-level comparisons that cross the late-May boundary mix different universe sizes — the rank numbers were mechanically diluted by the jump.
  • Cold-start gaps. Each ETF has a 30-day maturity ramp during which NEW flags are suppressed. The system started in February 2026; the leaderboard reaches its full signal density once every ETF is past the maturity gate.
