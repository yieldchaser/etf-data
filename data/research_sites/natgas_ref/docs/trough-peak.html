How to Use This Calculator
What is this calculator actually doing?

Simple idea: you trade a leveraged ETF cycle repeatedly, and this calculator compounds your wealth forward year by year — after every real cost: brokerage, FX markup, Indian LRS/TCS tax, STCG/LTCG slab, surcharge, cess. Every rupee figure you see is genuinely take-home, not gross.

Think of it as a flight simulator for your trade strategy. You set the controls, it simulates where you land after N years of compounding.

The core loop: Start with ₹X capital → trade a cycle → earn gross return → subtract broker fees + FX drag → subtract tax → repeat next year. The calculator does this swings/year × horizon years times and shows the result.
Step 1 — Auto vs Manual Mode

First decision: where do your numbers come from?

Seed from detected cycles (Auto): The calculator reads actual trough-to-peak cycles detected for your ticker and auto-fills Gross Return and Cycles/Year. Most grounded-in-reality starting point.

️ Manual override: Every number is yours to set freely. Ticker history is ignored. Use when you have your own return target in mind.

Start in Auto mode, then override specific fields as you get more opinionated about your strategy.

Step 2 — The Most Important Thing: Setting Your Return

This is where most confusion happens. There are three completely different ways to tell the calculator what return you expect per cycle — via the Gross Return / Cycle field:

AUTO — "Use the historical data as my base"

The Capture slider sets your fraction — how much of the full trough-to-peak move you realistically caught. It is NOT your return directly.

Formula: Gross Return = Historical Median × Capture %

Example: KOLD has an 86% median cycle. Set Capture = 60%.
→ Gross return = 86% × 60% = 51.7% per cycle — not 60%.

️ Most common misunderstanding. The slider multiplies the historical median — it doesn't set your return directly.
SINGLE — "I'm targeting exactly X% per trade"

Type a number. Type 60 → every cycle is exactly 60% gross. Capture slider disables. Ticker history becomes irrelevant.

Use this when you have a specific return target in your head and want to model that directly.
VARIABLE — "My trades have different return targets"

Set individual targets: Swing 1 = 60%, Swing 2 = 80%. The pattern repeats across the full horizon — Year 1 gets [60, 80], Year 2 gets [60, 80], and so on.

Use + / − buttons to add or remove swings from the pattern (up to 10).
Step 3 — Cycles/Year vs Swings/Year (they're different!)
Cycles/Year → physical reality (how many cycles FIT in a year historically) Swings/Year → your intention (how many you ACTUALLY plan to trade)

Cycles/Year comes from data: if a cycle + gap averages 146 days, then 365 ÷ 146 = 2.5 cycles/year. This is the physical ceiling.

Swings/Year is your actual frequency. Maybe you only catch 2 out of 3 available cycles — set it to 2. The simulation uses your intended frequency.

Fractional swings are intentional. 2.5 swings/year over 4 years = 10 total swings, where some years get 2 and others get 3. The Swing Schedule table below the chart shows exactly which years get which — no guessing.
Step 4 — Capital Risked / Trade

Do you put your entire corpus into each trade? Default is 100%. Set to 80% → "I deploy 80% per trade, 20% stays in cash."

Gain = corpus × risked% × net_return × swings/yr Example: ₹20L × 80% × 45% net × 2 swings = ₹14.4L gain in Year 1

Lower risked% → smaller gains, smaller tax bills. Useful for conservative scenario testing.

Step 5 — Tax, TCS & Vault (India-specific)

Holding Period — STCG vs LTCG:

STCG (<24 months): 30% base + surcharge (10–25%) + 4% cess → up to 39% effective LTCG (>24 months): 12.5% base + surcharge capped at 15% + 4% cess → up to ~15% effective

Cycles weeks-to-months long → STCG. Hold >24 months → LTCG. LTCG can nearly double your terminal wealth at high corpus levels — the rate difference is massive.

TCS Drag: India collects 20% TCS on LRS remittances above ₹10L/year. Refundable at tax time — but locked and can't compound during the year. TCS Drag ON models this real drag. Turn it OFF if you're under ₹10L or pre-funded.

Vault ON → profits stay offshore, TCS only on initial remittance. Vault OFF → annual repatriation, TCS hits each year's gains too. Vault ON is almost always the better long-term assumption.

The Swing Schedule Table

Below the chart is a year-by-year breakdown of your exact trade plan. Especially useful with fractional swings or Variable returns.

Example: 2.5 swings/year over 8 years → 20 total swings. Year 1 gets 2, year 2 gets 3, year 3 gets 2... With Variable returns [60%, 70%], you see exactly which swing gets which return, globally numbered.

Colour-coded return tags (cyan / gold / purple) map to each swing's position in your Variable pattern. Hover any tag for a tooltip with the exact global swing number.
Saving & Sharing Your Scenario

Share: Copies a URL encoding your entire setup — ticker, capital, return mode, variable values, swings/year, horizon, tax settings. Anyone who opens it gets your exact scenario. Great for saving your work or comparing strategies.

Download CSV: Exports the full year-by-year projection — corpus, gain, tax, effective rate, broker cost per year. Open in Excel or Google Sheets for further analysis.

Reset ↺: Every field has a small reset icon. Click it to restore just that field to its auto value without touching anything else.
